Choices the guide didn't make

Each one is a call made while drawing. Accept or reject one by one.

  1. TV "set" panel. The guide makes --ink the stage background and gives cards an --ink outline and shadow, but ink on ink is invisible. So the TV fills its 5% safe margin with --ink and puts a big --stage panel (40 px corners) over the safe area. Cards sit on that panel, where the outline and shadow show.
  2. Caption strip look. An --ink bar with 28 px corners: the talking grin, "SALTY" in --hot, the line in paper at 38 px. It sits just above the player tiles. Audio tags show as a muted italic stage direction, e.g. (sighs), never in brackets.
  3. Focus ring placement. A 6 px --spot outline set 6 px outside the card's 4 px ink border, so a dark gap separates ring and card even on yellow cards.
  4. Spice indicator. Four chilli icons, filled up to the level, plus the level name. When it's capped it also gets a lock and the reason ("— a little one's playing").
  5. Age-band icons. The same blob drawn bigger for each band (3–5 smallest, Grown-up biggest). No emoji, no birthdays.
  6. Admin marker. A crown badge on the admin's TV tile and "You run this room" on their phone.
  7. Phone answer layout. Four full-width buttons stacked top to bottom: dark card, coloured shape, answer text. I avoided a 2×2 grid of solid colour blocks so it can't be mistaken for the game we're not copying.
  8. Locked-in flood colour. The screen floods with the colour of the slot you picked (not your player colour), with its shape shown, so you can check your pick at a glance.
  9. Player tint. Phone background = 80% --ink mixed with 20% player colour. That's dark enough for paper text and light enough for ink shadows to show.
  10. Lobby with fewer than 10 players. Joined tiles are wide. The empty seats collapse into one dashed "+5 seats" tile instead of five empty ones.
  11. In-game tile status. "Locked in" with a --zap tick badge, "Thinking…" in muted, and "Turn next" in --spot for a Little whose picture question is coming up.
  12. Wrong answers at reveal drop to 45% opacity. The right one floods --zap with a paper ring and a tick, and the pickers' blobs sit on each row.
  13. Superstar badge. A separate yellow card next to the ranks, with the Little's blob big and sparkles. Their ranked row also gets a sparkle, so the rank is still honest but the celebration is louder.
  14. Pause menu. A flat 88% ink overlay (no blur, since the guide bans glass). The menu is Resume, Spice level and End game, as briefed. End game is in --hot, and Back resumes.
  15. Picture cards get pale pastel backgrounds (sky, pink, green tints) so the drawings read well and the ink-outlined animals pop. These pastels are new colours, not tokens.
  16. Kid-mode exit is a 3-second long-press on the top-left corner, with no visible handle on screen.
  17. Bleep style. A --hot pill with four ★ whatever the word's length, so the length doesn't give the word away. Shown next to the uncensored option (artboard 16).
  18. Filthy sample line. I used an exclamation ("Holy shit") rather than a swear-word noun aimed at the player ("you absolute ★★★★"), to stay clearly inside "punch up or punch himself".
  19. Game picker placeholders say "Coming soon / Game two / Game three", with no invented titles that might clash with real games.
  20. Grin construction. The colour mark is a --hot crescent with a --spot tooth and a paper brow. The one-colour mark cuts the tooth out as a notch. The talking version opens the mouth (ink inside) and raises the brow.
  21. Room code "ZUMP" and nicknames Toast, Noodle, Pickle, Zed, Blue Blob and Gravy are all made up.

Where the guide was unclear or contradicts itself

Resolved in the design guide on 2026-11-26: the stage panel (1), Little cards at about 28% width (2), two rows of tiles for 7+ players (3), a Little's question as its own turn (4), "nincompoop" removed (5), a new Filthy example aimed at the situation (6), the cap lifting when the youngest leaves (7), the PG-13 level renamed Hot (8), the pause menu (10), the cross-reference (11), and captions on the TV only (12). The bands are Little 3–5, Kids 6–8, Tweens 9–12, Teens 13–17 and Grown-ups 18+ (ADR 0008).

  1. Ink shadows on an ink stage (§5.2 vs §5.4): the outline and offset shadow vanish on the --ink background. Choice 1 works around it.
  2. The Little's picture cards can't all fit. Three cards at "at least 30% of the screen width" take 1728 px, which is the whole 5% safe area with no gaps and no room for the 1.08 focus scale. Artboard 05 keeps 576 px cards and lets the row run past the safe margin (60 px each side). The guide needs to pick one: 28% cards, or allow Little cards past the margin.
  3. Ten tiles and 32 px names don't fit. 10 tiles across 1728 px leaves about 150 px each, and a name like "Blue Blob" at 32 px doesn't fit next to an avatar. The guide needs a rule: two rows, shorter names, or a smaller tile with names on focus.
  4. "One thing on screen at a time" vs "older players keep playing while the Little's question waits" (§6 TV rule 2 vs Little rule 3). It's unclear whether the TV splits the screen or runs the Little's turn in sequence. These mockups run them in sequence.
  5. "Nincompoop" is allowed at Spicy, but rule 1 forbids mocking intelligence as a person. Calling a player a nincompoop sits on that line. Artboard 06 uses it (it's in the guide's own list); decide whether it stays.
  6. The Filthy example ("you absolute ***") aims the insult at the player, and it mentions "a picture of a cow", which is a Little-seat question, in a room that has to be all adults.
  7. Mild needs a Little, but the brief has a Spicy reveal and a Little on the scoreboard. They can't be one room, so artboard 06 says Blue Blob has gone to bed. The guide doesn't say whether the level rises on its own when the Little leaves, or waits for a grown-up.
  8. "Salty" is both the host and the PG-13 level, which made the admin slider and captions confusing to label.
  9. Bands are undefined. --grape is "the Kids band", but the guide never says which ages "Kids" covers, and there are five join choices against bands called Little, Kids and others. Filthy needs "every player 18+", but the top band is just "Grown-up"; I read Grown-up as 18+.
  10. Pause menu contents: §6 says "resume, settings, end game", but the brief says Resume / Spice level / End game. The caption on/off switch (§7) has no stated home either.
  11. Wrong cross-reference: §2 says the answer slots are in §5.4, but they're in §5.2.
  12. Captions on the phone: §7 puts captions on the TV only. The Filthy brief wanted Salty's line on the phone's result screen, and the guide doesn't say whether phones show his lines.
  13. Fonts: the guide says the fonts are self-hosted with no CDN at play time. These mockups load them from Google Fonts, which is fine for a design file only.
